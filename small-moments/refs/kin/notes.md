# Kin — customer support for small teams
Refs: sources.txt · motion strips: ../_motion/motion_a.jpg (rows 047, 048, 000)

## What the research showed
01 geometric-characters-login — overlapping flat shapes with dot eyes: the cutest result for the least ink. Our base.
02 blob-eyes-close-on-password — eyes track the caret while typing email and close to ^ ^ for password. A proven, loved pattern.
03 dog-covers-eyes — the peek on "show password" is the moment people screenshot.
04 owl-head-tracks-cursor — head and eyes move separately (head lags, eyes lead), which makes it feel alive.
05 support-mobile-light — a support home is a queue: count, next up, list. No dashboards.
06 support-mobile-tickets — ticket rows need status and age at a glance.
07 ai-support-copilot — a suggested-reply chip on the conversation preview.
08 minimal-line-character — personality from posture, not detail.

## Signature: "the crew"
Four teammates as simple shapes (coral circle, butter rounded square, mint pill, sky arch) standing shoulder to shoulder.
- Eyes follow the pointer; while you type an email, all four lean in and their pupils track the caret.
- Password: they close their eyes and look away. Show password: the smallest one peeks with one eye.
- Error: they glance at each other, then at the field, and shake their heads.
- Success: they hop in a wave, left to right.
- Tap a character: it squishes and says hello in a bubble.
- Idle: random blinks and an occasional glance at a neighbour.
- On Home the crew becomes the team; swipe a ticket to resolve it and its owner cheers.

## 5 rules
1. Characters are one shape + two eyes. A mouth appears only when reacting.
2. Pastel fills, ink pupils, never outlines.
3. Bodies lag behind eyes; secondary motion makes it feel alive.
4. Support copy is human and specific (names, times).
5. One accent action per screen (the ink button); everything else is soft.
