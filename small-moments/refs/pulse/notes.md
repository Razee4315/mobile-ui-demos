# Pulse — uptime monitoring SaaS
Refs: sources.txt

## What the research showed
01 server-monitor-light — light monitoring works when numbers are big and charts are hairline.
02 uptime-app-mobile — a mobile monitor is a list of services, each with a sparkline.
03 status-bars — 90-day uptime bars are the most recognisable status pattern.
04 status-page-calm — "All systems operational" as one calm sentence.
05 response-chart — a soft area chart with one highlighted point.
06 uptime-monitor-classic — incident timeline below the chart.
07 light-line-charts — thin lines, no grid, generous whitespace.
08 sleep-monitor-soft — health-app softness applied to infrastructure makes it less stressful.

## Signature: the heartbeat
A single mint signal line runs across the sign-in screen continuously (scrolling ECG).
- Typing: each keystroke spikes the line at the leading edge, and the response readout ticks.
- Password: the signal turns into an encrypted dot-dash pattern.
- Error: a soft coral irregular blip, plus an "incident" pill.
- Success: the line calms into a smooth wave before Home opens with the same line as its chart.
- Touch the chart on Home: a scrubber snaps to the nearest point with a readout; uptime bars show tooltips.
- Region dots ping like sonar.

## 5 rules
1. Mostly white; mint = healthy, amber = degraded, coral = down. Nothing else gets colour.
2. Hairline charts (1.5px), no gridlines, numbers in Martian Mono.
3. The status sentence is the hero; charts support it.
4. Motion always means data: anything that moves represents a signal.
5. Transitions are scan-line wipes, like a monitor refreshing.
